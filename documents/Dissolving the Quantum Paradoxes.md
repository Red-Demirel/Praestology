The CHSH inequality is the standard test for local realism. It states that for any local hidden variable theory, the sum of four correlation expectation values is bounded by $2$:

$$S = \vert{}E(A,B) - E(A,B') + E(A',B) + E(A',B')\vert{} \le 2$$

$$\langle B_A \cdot B_B \rangle = -\cos(\theta_A - \theta_B)$$

$$S = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) - \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) + \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) + \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)$$

$$S = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828$$

This is the Tsirelson bound—the maximum quantum mechanical violation of the CHSH inequality. It is not a mysterious limit; it is the natural consequence of the non-commutative geometry of bivector rotors acting on a shared uncommitted state.
