# Part 1: The Geometry of Uncommitted States

## From Phase Configuration Space to the First Closure

In standard quantum mechanics, a system in superposition is written as a vector in a complex Hilbert space:

$$\vert{}\psi\rangle = \alpha\vert{}0\rangle + \beta\vert{}1\rangle$$

This notation is computationally effective, but it is physically silent: it tells us how to calculate probabilities, but it does not tell us what the field is doing before a measurement occurs. The **Nested Closure Hierarchy** replaces this abstract formalism with **Geometric Algebra (\(Cl(3,0)\))**—the coordinate-free language of real 3D space. In $Cl(3,0)$, space is not a passive container filled with point particles; it is a continuous multivector field composed of distinct geometric grades: scalars (magnitude), vectors (direction), bivectors (oriented planes), and pseudoscalars (oriented volumes). Superposition, entanglement, and measurement are not mysterious paradoxes; they are the deterministic geometry of an uncommitted multivector field at Depth 0 ($D_0$) resolving its internal constraints into a discrete topological closure at Depth 1 ($D_1$).

## Depth 0: The Phase Configuration Space as Vector-Potential

The foundational substrate of the hierarchy is the **phase configuration space**—the impulse-driven domain of pure potential, described by $Cl(3,0)$. This is not a void; it is a continuous distribution of unresolved bivector orientations, a field of geometric potential that has not yet committed to any specific plane.

### The General Bivector

The bivector is the fundamental element of rotational geometry, spin, and phase in $Cl(3,0)$. It represents an oriented 2D plane, distinct from a vector (a 1D line). A general bivector $B$ is expressed as a linear combination of spatial basis planes:

$$B = b_{12}(e_1 \wedge e_2) + b_{13}(e_1 \wedge e_3) + b_{23}(e_2 \wedge e_3)$$

Its magnitude, given by:

$$\vert{}B\vert{}^2 = b_{12}^2 + b_{13}^2 + b_{23}^2$$

when normalised to the unit sphere ($\vert{}B\vert{}^2 = 1$), parameterises all possible rotational planes in 3D space. At Depth 0, the bivector field is uncommitted; it does not possess a single, fixed spatial plane. Instead, it exists in a state of **orientational diffuseness** across the unit sphere. Superposition is not a particle in two places at once; it is a continuous distribution of the bivector across the sphere—a geometric state of unresolved potential.

## Depth 1: The First Closure as Vector-Expression

A system cannot remain uncommitted indefinitely. As local field gradients interact or encounter spatial boundaries, the uncommitted bivector field undergoes an **R5 commitment event**, locking into a specific unit bivector orientation ($\hat{B}$). Upon commitment, the field executes a spatial transformation described by a **rotor**:

$$R = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \hat{B} \theta \right) = \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) - \hat{B} \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$

To achieve dynamic stability, the rotor must satisfy the **Closure Condition**:

$$Q^* = \mathcal{R}_k[Q^*]$$

For a spin-1/2 state, this invariance requires a full $720^\circ$ ($\theta = 4\pi$) spatial winding—a **Möbius rotor**:

$$R(4\pi) = +1$$

This single condition governs the emergence of Depth 1 persistent structures:

* **The Electron:** A fully closed, self-invariant $720^\circ$ Möbius rotor, stable and localised.


* **The Photon:** A counter-oriented bivector pair ($L + R$) with net zero chirality, propagating continuously without forming a localized closure.


* **The Quark:** A partial topological winding (stopped at $360^\circ$), confined to multi-rotor triality closures at Depth 2.



The rotor is the first vector-expression: the committed, set state that emerges from the uncommitted vector-potential of Depth 0.

## Entanglement: Shared Vector-Potential

Entanglement, often treated as a non-local correlation, is reframed in this hierarchy as **shared vector-potential**. Two or more structures share a common unresolved bivector configuration at Depth 0; their orientations are coupled because they are part of the same continuous distribution. When one structure undergoes R5 commitment, it does not "collapse" the other; it selects a specific orientation from the shared potential, leaving the remaining structure still coupled to the unresolved residue. The correlation is not mysterious; it is the geometry of a shared field.

## The Transitional Stage: From Depth 1 to Depth 2

The rotor at Depth 1 is not the end of the process; it is the beginning of the next stage. A committed rotor becomes a vector-potential for the next depth. When two or more rotors couple via shared bivector potential, they form a new, higher-order uncommitted configuration at Depth 2. The triality closure (proton, neutron) is the vector-expression of this shared potential—the satisfaction of the closure condition $Q^* = \mathcal{R}_k[Q^*]$ at the next depth.

This is the **relational bridge**: the same operation, repeated. The vector-potential at Depth $N$ becomes the vector-expression at Depth $N+1$. The hierarchy is not a sequence of separate theories; it is a single, iterated process.

The vector states—from the continuous distribution of the bivector to its commitment in a stable rotor—map directly onto the R1–R5 sequence:

| **R1 (Existence)** | The phase configuration space exists | Pure potential, prior to any commitment. |
| **R2 (Distinction)** | The bivector emerges | A specific oriented plane is distinguished from the continuous distribution—the "unset" state. |

The diffuse state is the pre‑commitment regime (R1–R3); the set state is the post‑commitment regime (R4–R5). The transition from diffuse to set is the R5 commitment event itself.

## Summary: Potential, Expression, and the Relational Bridge

The Nested Closure Hierarchy is built on a single, iterated operation: the transition from vector-potential to vector-expression, mediated by the closure condition $Q^* = \mathcal{R}_k[Q^*]$. At Depth 0, the vector-potential is the uncommitted bivector distribution; at Depth 1, the vector-expression is the $720^\circ$ Möbius rotor. Entanglement is shared vector-potential; the transitional stage is the relational bridge that connects one depth to the next.

Superposition is not a particle in two places; it is the continuous orientational diffuseness of the bivector. Measurement is not a collapse; it is an R5 commitment event that pins the bivector to a specific orientation. Physical stability is not a postulate; it is the satisfaction of the closure condition.

The rotor is the first closure, but the process does not stop at Depth 1. In **Part 2**, we will examine how shared bivector coupling at Depth 0 dissolves the paradoxes of quantum entanglement and non-locality, and how the same operation scales to produce the triality closure at Depth 2.
